Tito Ortiz vs Sea Monster: The Show Where a Legend Trades the Octagon for the Ocean

By Cara Leopardo, Founder & CEO · July 9, 2026

If you’ve ever started a show with your kids, gotten four minutes in, and then dove across the couch for the remote because somebody on screen said something you’re now going to be explaining at dinner — this one’s for you.

We have four kids. We’ve made that dive more times than we can count. It’s the entire reason we built We The Studios in the first place: nobody was making TV for families anymore, so we did.

The shows below are the kind you can put on without a pre-screen, a hovering thumb on the remote, or that low-grade dread in your stomach. Real entertainment — funny, sweet, occasionally great — minus the profanity, the suggestive content, and the stuff that ages a kid five years in thirty seconds.

What “no bad language” should actually mean

Here’s the problem with most “family-friendly” labels: they’re a vibe, not a standard. A show gets called family-friendly because it has a kid in it, and then somewhere in episode three the dialogue turns and you’re scrambling.

When we say a show is safe at We The Studios, we mean a real line in the sand: no profanity, no sexual content, and no glorifying alcohol. Not “mostly.” Not “depends on the episode.” Every series, every episode. That’s the whole promise — you shouldn’t have to keep auditing.

Tito Ortiz vs Sea Monster

Let’s be honest: most people know Tito Ortiz from the Ultimate Fighting Championship — where he spent years throwing hands in the octagon with guys twice his size.

But ask him what he really loves?

“I consider myself a better fisherman than a fighter.”

That’s the heart of this show: it’s not about combat, it’s about connection — to nature, to family, and to a life he built before the lights went on.

In every episode, Tito trades his gloves for his fishing poles and invites someone (often Curtis, his longtime friend) out into the Gulf waters off Southwest Florida. Their mission? Catch goliath grouper — 8-foot, 800-pound sea monsters that live in shipwrecks and can drag a boat for miles if you don’t hold on tight.

This isn’t an action show. It’s a quiet, powerful reminder that the strongest men often fight their hardest battles far from crowds — with a rod in hand, a storm rolling in, and no backup plan but muscle memory, faith, and trust in your crew.

Why this is safe for everyone (even the eye-rolling tweens)

When we say a show is safe at We The Studios, we mean a real line in the sand: no profanity, no sexual content, and no glorifying alcohol. Not "mostly." Not "depends on the episode." Every series, every episode.

Tito Ortiz vs Sea Monster passes with room to spare:

  • Language? Clean, heartfelt — occasional “real real real real” excitement, but nothing offensive or crude.
  • Themes? Patience (waiting for the bite), legacy (teaching Curtis how to fish), respect for nature (releasing every fish unharmed), and fatherhood (Tito reflects on moving from California to Florida to protect his kids’ future).
  • Tone? Chill, observant, sometimes humorous (“I hope he can pull on these things hard, and they’ll try to pull him in the water”). There’s no yelling, no sabotage — just shared effort and wonder at what lives beneath.

The tween who rolls their eyes at “babyish” shows won’t walk away here. This isn’t about talking down to families — it’s about standing beside them. And that respect? It translates across ages.

Clean doesn’t mean dull. It just means you wrote the story without leaning on shock to keep people watching.

The moment that proves it

Midway through the episode, Tito and Curtis hook goliath grouper simultaneously — their lines tangled in a shipwreck. The captain yells instructions: “Over here! Over under! You’re underneath!”

They untangle. They reset. And Curtis lands his first-ever saltwater catch — a 300-pounder.

Tito doesn’t rage or boast. He smiles and says:

“I got to give kudos to Curtis. Awesome job. Proud of him.”

Tito Ortiz

Then, when they get back to shore:

“That was awesome… I think it was a perfect day. Tito Ortiz got his sea monster.”

Tito Ortiz

He doesn’t claim the biggest fish — he celebrates that Curtis got one too.

This is the magic. This isn’t a fishing show. It’s about what happens when you take pride not in winning, but in helping others succeed.

What to expect in every episode

  • A boat, some heavy tackle, and zero guarantees (the fish decide when they bite)
  • Tito teaching someone how to fight sea monsters — while quietly reflecting on fatherhood, faith, and freedom
  • Every fish caught is released unharmed (no killing, no trophies — just the story of the catch)
  • Curtis or another guest learning what it means to hold on tight (literally and emotionally)

You don’t need to vet it. You don’t need to hover. You can just… watch.

Because when you get home from work, the last thing you want is another show you have to manage.
