Get the model right first

YouTube is marketing.
The platform is the business.

Sneak peeks, 8-minute unlocks, trailers on YouTube · Instagram (largest social) · paid social. Zero barrier to entry. Blake Reynolds is the emotional hook.

Bottom of funnel

wethestudios.com

$3.98/month or $29.98/year. New episodes every Sunday. One of the lowest conversion barriers in streaming — less than a coffee.

The asset

Owned subscribers

Recurring revenue WTS controls. Not algorithm-dependent CPM. Every framework in this deck measures progress toward one number: paying subs.

Proof of model: The Chosen made Season 1 free during COVID — revenue went up, not down. 280M viewers from free top-of-funnel feeding a deeper platform experience. Netflix and Disney+ (Andor S1 on YouTube) run the same play.

The breakeven math

The number is 5,300.

Breakeven subscriber count. Roughly the population of one small Kentucky town — Blake's town, fittingly, is 2,700. You need two of them.

Churn makes it a treadmill: at 5% monthly churn, holding 5,300 subs requires ~265 new subs every month just to stand still. Building to 5,500 by month 24 requires ~390 new subs/month average — exactly what the benchmark funnel on slide 3 produces.

The content engine

Blake Reynolds is the hook,
not the business

  • Raising Reynolds is the flagship funnel asset — the most emotionally accessible show for the exact audience the platform serves. The Chosen made one person feel like Jesus was their friend; the sneak peek engine does that for Blake, one free episode at a time.
  • Cadence: two sneak peeks per month per show in pilot, scaling across all five properties once the template is proven — thumbnails, titles, cut structure, CTA placement.
  • Every video ends with the ask — a direct title card to wethestudios.com, not a fade-out. At $3.98 the only thing between a leaned-in viewer and a subscription is a frictionless CTA.
  • YouTube CPM on free content is a marketing rebate. It reduces effective CAC. It never appears as a revenue line in the model, so nobody optimizes for the wrong thing.
  • The 8-minute unlock on episode one of each series removes the last trust barrier — viewers commit to a story before they ever see a paywall.

Early warning system

Kill metrics that pre-empt the gates

  • View-to-sub below 0.02% after 6 months of iteration — the funnel concept itself is broken, not the execution. That's a model failure, not a tactics failure.
  • Monthly churn above 8% sustained — people sample and leave. Content-market fit problem; no amount of top-of-funnel spend fixes it.
  • CAC payback over 6 months — kill the paid channel immediately, survive on organic, reassess at the next gate.
  • Sunday drop cadence breaks repeatedly — retention follows reliability. A streaming service that misses release dates churns itself.
  • Curtis or Cara signal deprioritization — the honest conversation happens immediately. Nobody runs a zombie platform.

The non-negotiables

Five rules. Then the order fills or it doesn't.

  1. The platform is the business. Every piece of free content exists to move someone toward wethestudios.com. No orphan content.
  2. Free feeds paid. The Chosen proved it; giving the best moments away grows revenue. Never hoard the hook behind the paywall.
  3. Measure the funnel, not the noise. Views are diagnostic. Subs, churn, and CAC payback are decisional.
  4. Annual plans are the churn weapon. Bias every checkout toward $29.98/year. Twelve months of guaranteed retention per conversion.
  5. Kill decisions are clean. A missed gate executes immediately. There is no "next month will be better."

The funnel doesn't create demand for family entertainment — it measures it. And by month 24 it will have said, in subscriber arithmetic nobody can argue with, whether the demand is there.
