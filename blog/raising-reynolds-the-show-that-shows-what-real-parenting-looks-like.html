Raising Reynolds: The Show That Shows What Real Parenting Looks Like

By Cara Leopardo, Founder & CEO · July 9, 2026

If you’ve ever started a show with your kids, gotten four minutes in, and then dove across the couch for the remote because somebody on screen said something you’re now going to be explaining at dinner — this one’s for you.

We have four kids. We’ve made that dive more times than we can count. It’s the entire reason we built We The Studios in the first place: nobody was making TV for families anymore, so we did.

The shows below are the kind you can put on without a pre-screen, a hovering thumb on the remote, or that low-grade dread in your stomach. Real entertainment — funny, sweet, occasionally great — minus the profanity, the suggestive content, and the stuff that ages a kid five years in thirty seconds.

What “no bad language” should actually mean

Here’s the problem with most “family-friendly” labels: they’re a vibe, not a standard. A show gets called family-friendly because it has a kid in it, and then somewhere in episode three the dialogue turns and you’re scrambling.

When we say a show is safe at We The Studios, we mean a real line in the sand: no profanity, no sexual content, and no glorifying alcohol. Not “mostly.” Not “depends on the episode.” Every series, every episode. That’s the whole promise — you shouldn’t have to keep auditing.

Raising Reynolds

This is parenting, unfiltered. Blake Reynolds — builder, social media influencer, husband, dad of four adopted kids — pulls no punches in showing what it really looks like to raise children who weren’t born to you.

Season 1 drops us straight into the chaos: it’s Saturday morning, and Blake needs a chicken coop built before the heat sets in. The kids are notcooperating (as usual). He’s already sweating, he’s running low on patience, and he’s not above yelling “*All right, y’all do the rest!**” to his wife Emily.

But this isn’t about choreography. It’s about principles. About teaching your kids how to build something with their own hands. About explaining why raising mixed-race children in Kentucky requires intention — not just love. About calling your son out when he raises his hand at another adult (“*You put me in a situation I don’t need to be in, okay?**”) because respect isn’t optional.

And yes — there’s the occasional “y’all,” “suck,” and even a moment where Blake admits he’s “running low on patience.” But there’s no profanity. No inappropriate content. Just real people being real parents — sometimes patient, often frustrated, always trying.

Why this is safe for everyone (even the eye-rolling tweens)

When we say a show is safe at We The Studios, we mean a real line in the sand: no profanity, no sexual content, and no glorifying alcohol. Not "mostly." Not "depends on the episode." Every series, every episode.

Raising Reynolds passes with room to spare:

  • Language? “Y’all,” “suck,” occasional frustration — but zero actual curse words or slurs.
  • Themes? Adoption, blended families (Blake and Emily’s story is deeply moving), faith, responsibility, teaching work ethic. It’s not sugarcoated — Blake admits he’s “running low on patience” and talks openly about financial pressures and parenting doubts.
  • Tone? Warm, direct, sometimes chaotic — but never exploitative. The drama is real, not manufactured for clicks.

The tween who rolls their eyes at “babyish” shows won’t walk away here. This isn’t about talking down to families — it’s about standing beside them. And that respect? It translates across ages.

Clean doesn’t mean dull. It just means you wrote the story without leaning on shock to keep people watching.

The moment that proves it

Midway through Season 1, Blake explains why he’s hard on his son Kingston:

“If I’m gone tomorrow, you got to pick up the slack.”

Blake Reynolds

Then he asks Kingston what he thinks — why does Dad treat him so strictly? Kingston says:

“Reason he cut his dad was hard on him. So now he treated us like he… his dad gave him his to be hard on his kids. But what’s the best to raise us, right?”

Kingston Reynolds

Blake doesn’t shy away from the question. He answers honestly: he wants his kids to be productive members of society, not entitled heirs.

“I’m not your friend. I’m here to be your parent. I will be your friend. And I want you to be my friend. I just want to help mold and shape you into a good human.”

Blake Reynolds

That line — spoken by a father who built houses, adopted four kids, and is still figuring it out every day — is the heart of this show.

What to expect in every episode

  • A family building (or fixing) something practical — chicken coops, houses, boats
  • Real conversations about race, adoption, faith, money, and parenting doubts
  • Kids calling each other out, playing pranks, and occasionally shooting drones out of the sky
  • Blake losing patience… then resetting, taking a breath, and trying again

You don’t need to vet it. You don’t need to hover. You can just… watch.

Because when you get home from work, the last thing you want is another show you have to manage.
