Mr. Miami & Me: The Show for Every Dad Trying to Hold It All Together

By Cara Leopardo, Founder & CEO · July 10, 2026

If you've ever started a show with your kids, gotten four minutes in, and then dove across the couch for the remote because someone on screen said something you're now going to be explaining at dinner — this one's for you.

We have four kids. We've made that dive more times than we can count. It's the entire reason we built We The Studios in the first place: nobody was making TV for families anymore, so we did.

The shows below are the kind you can put on without a pre-screen, a hovering thumb on the remote, or that low-grade dread in your stomach. Real entertainment — funny, sweet, occasionally great — minus the profanity, the suggestive content, and the stuff that ages a kid five years in thirty seconds.

Mr. Miami & Me

This is the show for father-daughter duos, single-parent households, and anyone who knows what it looks like to try to build a business while still showing up at pickup with clean socks.

Jacob's a dad running a med spa in Miami — part clinic, part production studio, 100% juggling. His days look like:

  • Breakfast with Abigail (her mornings start slow; his do not)
  • Morning huddle with 17 clients lined up
  • Amex billing panic ("Wait… I used the wrong account number?")
  • Prepping a client who lost her sight and hearing in brain tumor surgery
  • Then, as dusk hits — writing lyrics with his daughter about self-worth

There's no script. There's just life — messy, urgent, and beautiful.

Why this is safe for everyone (even the eye-rolling tweens)

When we say a show is safe at We The Studios, we mean a real line in the sand: no profanity, no sexual content, and no glorifying alcohol. Not "mostly." Not "depends on the episode." Every series, every episode.

Mr. Miami & Me passes with room to spare:

  • Language? Clean. You'll hear "gosh," "man," and more than one "sorry, sorry, sorry."
  • Themes? Resilience (Abigail's comeback), creativity (her songwriting), integrity (Jacob turning away patients he can't help).
  • Tone? Warm, sometimes chaotic — but never cynical. Jacob's the kind of dad who helps his daughter braid her hair before school and then reopens a client's paralyzed facial nerves.

The tween who rolls their eyes at "babyish" shows won't walk away here. Abigail is on TikTok, makes beats, writes songs, and runs the social media — not as a prop, but as an equal voice in the business.

Clean doesn't mean dull. It just means you wrote the story without leaning on shock to keep people watching.

The moment that proves it

Midway through Season 1, Jacob sits with Abigail after her dance rehearsal and school day. She's tired. He's juggling billing, product orders, and a client follow-up.

Then she drops this:

"You're pretty. You're strong. You were curious. Me? Anything you want…"

"Find the courage to stand by your power."

Abigail, Mr. Miami & Me

It's not polished. It's written in under an hour between homework and hair-styling. But it lands. Jacob's eyes well up.

Because this isn't a show about a dad fixing people's faces.

It's about a dad building someone else — his daughter — while trying to keep the lights on at his business.

That's the magic: you're not just watching Mr. Miami. You're seeing your own kitchen-table moment, played a little bigger and a lot more relatable.

What to expect in every episode

  • A family breakfast (Abigail eats Costa; Jacob does his hair)
  • A "let's get it" morning team huddle ("We may run out of product today…")
  • A client with a real story — not just aesthetic, but emotional
  • A billing panic or two (it's Miami — chaos comes free)
  • And always, a moment where Jacob looks at Abigail and says: "I couldn't be more proud."

You don't need to vet it. You don't need to hover. You can just… watch.

Because when you get home from work, the last thing you want is another show you have to manage.
