The premise

The toolkit problem

Standard MBA frameworks miss this business.

FrameworkWhy it fails here
Lean CanvasAssumes you're solving a customer problem. WTS sells entertainment — identity and connection, not pain relief.
Business Model CanvasToo generic. Doesn't model algorithm dependence or fragmented monetization.
VC MethodIrrelevant. Nobody is raising. Burn comes from personal capital.

The right toolkit is a creator-economy stack: audience is the asset, the algorithm is the channel, monetization is fragmented across small streams. Five layers, next.

Layer 2 · Execution — the heartbeat

Pirate Metrics, creator edition

If any one of these five trends wrong for two consecutive months, something is broken. Fix it or kill it.

A
Acquisition

New subs/month. Growth targets: +30% MoM early, +15% mid, +8% mature.

A
Activation

≥60% of new subs watch a first video. Below that: thumbnails, titles, or audience mismatch.

R
Retention

≥40% watch 2+ videos. The single best predictor of long-term viability.

R

$/subscriber/month, broken by stream. Vanity views don't count.

R

≥40% organic. If the audience isn't evangelizing, the content isn't shareable.

Early warning system

Red flags that pre-empt the gates

  • Subscriber growth flat 3+ months — pivot content immediately; flat 3 more → kill decision now, not at the next gate
  • Retention below 25% — stop all paid acquisition the same day; content-audience mismatch must be fixed before another dollar moves
  • Burn above $20K/mo — freeze hiring, cut marketing; if burn doesn't drop in 30 days, escalate to kill
  • Zero licensing interest by M9 — hire an agent or formally abandon the stream and reallocate
  • Curtis signals burnout or deprioritization — the honest conversation happens immediately. Nobody forces a zombie project.

The non-negotiables

Five rules. Then the order fills or it doesn't.

  1. Blake is the brand. No sponsor deal or license is worth poisoning subscriber trust.
  2. Consistency beats virality. A retained audience is worth 10× a viral spike that doesn't convert.
  3. Own the audience. YouTube owns the algorithm — Patreon and email are the insurance.
  4. One platform first. Own YouTube before touching anything else.
  5. Kill decisions are clean. A missed gate executes immediately. There is no "next month will be better."

The frameworks don't create audience attention — they measure it. And they say, without emotion, when to walk away.
